Hardware notes

Serial voltage, pinouts, reboot behaviour and what can and cannot be changed from the router.

Voltage warning

The router's serial header uses 3.3V TTL logic. Do not connect an RS232 adapter, which uses plus or minus 12V — it will destroy the board. Use a 3.3V USB-UART adapter and trace the pinout for your board revision before probing.

Serial / UART

  • Use uart-at --list to enumerate ports on this machine.
  • Use uart-at --port COM3 --script to preview the scripted AT sequence without opening the port.
  • Use uart-at --simulate --script to verify the AT engine end-to-end against an in-memory mock device.
  • Pinouts vary by board revision. Do not assume a layout from a photo of a different board.

Shell persistence

telnet starts a runtime-only telnetd on port 23. After a reboot, that shell is gone. Two options:

  • Rerun telnet after each reboot.
  • Run persist, which appends a boot block to /etc/rc (backed up to /etc/rc.zlt-backup) and enables the NVRAM flags for telnet, ADB and dropbear. After reboot, ports 23 and 22 open automatically.

ADB on this firmware binds only 127.0.0.1:5037 in host mode and needs USB. Network ADB on port 5555 is not available without additional firmware support.

RF and network limits

optimize prints band, bandwidth, RSRP/RSRQ/SINR, PCI and SIM PLMN, and changes only what the router controls: DNS, data-cap and quota alerts, QoS and band lock. Carrier-core latency from CGNAT or roaming cannot be changed from the device.

On the reference device the air interface was already healthy — Band 3, 20 MHz, SINR around 19 dB — so remaining latency was in the operator's network.

Physical risk

Enabling ports, writing NVRAM and pushing a TR-069 Download RPC can render the router unusable. Modifying firmware can permanently brick the device and void its warranty. Keep a recovery plan and only work on hardware you can afford to lose.
